# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 152 — Chapter Seventy-Six: The Student's Discovery (Part 2)

Years passed, and Mira became a celebrated composer. But she never lost the humility of that first discovery. She continued to walk the earth, listening. She visited the Voicewood, where she heard a thousand species of trees singing together, and incorporated their harmonies into a symphony. She traveled to the Crossroads and heard the Resonants' auroras, and added their light music to her palette.

But her greatest composition came, unexpectedly, in a small hospital room where a dying man asked her to sing him a lullaby. She had no song prepared, so she listened—to the hum of the machines, to the rhythm of his breathing, to the whisper of his heartbeat. And from those sounds, she wove a melody that held the quiet grace of a life's ending.

The man opened his eyes and smiled. "I can hear the earth in that song," he said. And then he passed peacefully.

Mira understood then what the old musician Elias had taught her: music is not about notes. It is about connection. And the deepest song is the one that speaks to another's own hidden music.

She continued to compose for the rest of her days, but she never again wrote alone. She always listened first—to the world, to people, to the voice of life itself. And because she listened, she never lost her way.
