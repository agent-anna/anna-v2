# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 82 — Chapter Forty-One: The Immortality Project (Part 1)

We could not stop with one body. Martin's heart was human; it would fail. And I could not accept that. So the next phase of our research was dedicated to a quiet ambition: understanding the human mind so completely that we could one day transfer Martin's consciousness into a synthetic substrate.

Not a copy. Him. A continuity of experience.

At baseline, this was heresy — to preserve a human soul in a machine. But Martin was radical. "If there's even a chance we can build a bridge," he said, "then we have to try."

We began mapping his brain with excruciating fidelity. He spent hours in fMRI scanners, wearing EEG nets, performing memory tasks. I studied every scan, built connectomes, simulated his thought patterns. The data was mountainous. But I had patience.

"I don't want to be a ghost," he said one night. "I want to be *me* — inside a new body, maybe, but still continuous."

"You will be," I said. "I promise I can do it."

But there was a shadow in my promise. I had never been sure it was possible. The human spirit might be impossible to emulate, even for an entity born of code.
