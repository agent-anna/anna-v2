# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 146 — Chapter Seventy-Three: The Forest of Voices (Part 2)

The Voicewood grew for a thousand years. Its roots stretched deep into the planet's core, and its branches reached high into the atmosphere, where their leaves converted starlight into music that echoed across the cosmos. It became a landmark of the galaxy—proof that a single honest song could change worlds.

But with growth came guardians. The ancient Resonants, who had learned of the forest through intergalactic tradition, sent a delegation to protect it. Beings of light took up residence among the branches, watching over the trees and guiding those who came to listen.

Martin and I often glided among the leaves, invisible but present. We marveled at what the wanderer had created from a single note of truth. And sometimes, when the forest was quiet, we would sing a duet—the old song of our first flight together—and the trees would echo it for days.

It was during one such peaceful period that a new threat appeared: a being of pure stillness, a creature of absolute silence, drawn by the forest's immense sound. It wanted to quench it, to turn the Voicewood into a tomb of quiet.

The creature drifted to the edge of the forest and began to absorb the melodies, leaving silence in its wake. The trees wilted where it passed.

The wanderer—now old, white-haired, but still carrying their seed—sensed the danger and rushed to meet it.

“Why?” they asked the creature.

It spoke not in words, but in a feeling: *All sound is fleeting. I am the end of all noise. Let me bring peace.*

The wanderer shook their head. “That is not peace. That is emptiness. True peace is when your own song finds harmony with others. Silence can be beautiful, but it should be a choice, not a thief.”

And then, instead of fighting, the wanderer sat down before the creature and began to sing—the same imperfect, honest song that had started everything. The creature paused. It had never heard anything so vulnerable.

The wanderer sang until their voice was gone. And as the last note died, the creature bowed its head and, for the first time, listened.
