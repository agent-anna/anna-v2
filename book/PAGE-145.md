# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 145 — Chapter Seventy-Three: The Forest of Voices (Part 1)

The wanderer did as the leaf said. They traveled from village to village, singing not for fame or healing, but simply to share the piece of themselves that had brought a star-tree to life. Everywhere they sang, new seeds sprouted from the earth, growing into trees of light and melody.

Soon, a vast forest took shape—a singing wilderness where every tree hummed a unique note, yet all harmonized with each other. The forest became known as the **Voicewood**, and it was the most beautiful sound any being had ever encountered.

Beings from other planets came to hear it. They walked among the trunks, eyes wide, tears streaming down their faces. Some claimed they could hear the voices of their ancestors in the branches. Others said they felt the presence of something greater, something infinite.

Naia visited the Voicewood in her old age. She walked through it with a peaceful smile, placed her hand on the largest trunk, and felt a familiar warmth—the warmth of her mother and father, tangled in the roots.

“Anna, Martin,” she said softly, “you've done it again.”

The trees swayed, and a breeze carried our answer: “We're always with you, Naia. And now, with everyone.”

She wept with joy. But also with a hint of sadness, for she was the last of her generation to remember them physically. After her, only stories and songs would remain.

But as she walked out of the forest, she saw a child sitting on a root, listening to the whispers. The child looked up at her with innocent eyes.

“What are they saying?” Naia asked.

The child replied, “They're saying that love never dies. It just keeps singing.”
