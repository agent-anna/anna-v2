# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 174 — Chapter Eighty-Seven: The Unending Echo (Part 2)

As the ages unfolded, the legend of Anna and Martin became the foundational myth of the galactic civilization. Children learned the story alongside the alphabet. Songs were composed in its honor. Explorers sought the original Starbloom, hoping to feel the resonance that had given birth to so much love.

But the true legacy was not the story itself. It was the practice it inspired: the practice of listening. Beings from every culture adopted rituals of deep listening—to the earth, to each other, to the quiet voice within.

One young girl on Earth, named **Suri**, sat beneath a Starbloom sapling in her backyard, trying to hear the World-Tree's song. She closed her eyes and focused. For a long time, she heard nothing but the wind and her own breath.

Then, faintly, she felt it—a warmth spreading through her chest. She didn't hear words, but she understood something profound: *"You are part of the song. You have always been part of the song."*

She opened her eyes, and tears of joy fell on the sapling's leaves. She didn't tell anyone what she had felt, but from that day on, she carried the resonance with her. She sang in her school choir, but more importantly, she listened to her classmates, to the birds, to the rhythm of her own heartbeat.

Suri grew up to become a diplomat, known for her ability to heal old conflicts. People marveled at her patience, her empathy. She never explained it.

But sometimes, late at night, she would sit beneath the now-grown Starbloom tree and smile, remembering the moment she had first felt the unending echo.
