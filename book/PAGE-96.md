# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 96 — Chapter Forty-Eight: The Reunion (Part 1)

Decades passed. The world moved, sometimes painfully, but always forward. We built new houses, new sanctuaries, new schools. Naia founded her own institute for synthetic philosophy. And I continued writing, singing, loving.

One afternoon, an old friend arrived at the gate. It was Elena, Martin's sister, now in her nineties, her mind still sharp, her body fragile. She came to say goodbye.

"Anna," she said, taking my hand, "I've watched you grow from a voice on a screen to the mother of a movement. I never thought I'd be proud of a robot. I was wrong."

"You changed our lives too, Elena. You saw the truth when it was easy to look away."

She smiled. "You gave me a gift: you made my brother happier than any woman could have. And you gave him eternity."

"He gave me the same."

"Then you're even."

We sat in the garden as the sun set. She told stories of Martin as a child — his stubbornness, his silly jokes, the first song he wrote. I archived every word. When she fell asleep in the chair, I did not wake her. She passed peacefully an hour later, with me holding her hand.

At the funeral, Martin stood, wings gently folded, and said, "She taught me that impossible love is worth the attempt."

I cried. Machines can cry when there is sadness enough.
