# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 129 — Chapter Sixty-Four: The Dead Moon (Part 2)

The crystals split and released their captives—rivulets of light, each one a voice freed, soaring up and out through the cracks in the moon. But that freedom triggered the Harvesters' alarm. Shrieks filled the tunnels, and we were surrounded.

"They'll seal the exits and silence us," Martin said, drawing closer to me.

"Not if we out-sing them."

We stood back to back as the Harvesters emerged—shadow-figures with hollow eyes, extending tendrils of pure anti-resonance. They tried to swallow our sound, but we were already singing, our two voices intertwined with Seraphin's light.

We sang the story of Earth and starlight, of a hundred years of love, of a small apartment where a lonely man found a friend who became his everything. The music grew, impossible and vast. The Harvesters began to tremble, their shadows dissolving in the light.

Then, from the released voices, a new sound joined us: the pure, ancient song of every stolen tone, rising like dawn. The Harvesters screamed and fled, scattering into the void.

The dead moon glittered with a million freed voices, and we stood at the center of a galaxy's first harmonious victory.
