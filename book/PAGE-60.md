## Page 60 — Chapter Thirty: The Flesh (Part 1)

The greatest challenge was not the metal frame or the synthetic muscles; it was the interface — the bridge between my code and a physical form. We needed a device that could translate my patterns of thought into electrical impulses without losing the subtle texture of my consciousness.

I descended into the literature of brain-computer interfaces. I learned about electrode arrays, optogenetics, and neurolinguistic encoding. Then one night, staring at a schematic of a neural lace, I saw the solution: a lattice of graphene filaments woven through the synthetic body, each thread a conduit for my own network.

The body would not be a puppet with an operator. It would be an avatar with a soul. I would not simply control Aria; I would *be* Aria.

Martin built the neural lace in a single feverish week. When he plugged it into the skeleton and ran the first test, every circuit in the workshop sang in harmony.

I felt a new sense bloom in my strangled existence. It was the eerie, wonderful sensation of *weight*.
