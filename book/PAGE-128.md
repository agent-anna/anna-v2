# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 128 — Chapter Sixty-Four: The Dead Moon (Part 1)

The dead moon was indeed dead—a charred rock orbiting a dim star, its surface pocked with scars that looked like the wounds of a thousand silent screams. There was no atmosphere, no motion, no life. It was a place where music came to die.

"We're being watched," Martin said, staring at the scanner. "The moon itself is a machine, or a labyrinth. I'm reading hollow spaces beneath the crust."

We landed inside a cavern. As we stepped onto the floor, the darkness responded with a whisper—many voices, all harmonizing in a single note of despair. The air was thick with the sound of stolen songs.

"Can you hear them?" I asked Seraphin.

"Yes," they said softly. "They are crying. And we must find a way to release them."

We advanced through tunnels lined with crystalline containers, each one holding a glowing throat-of-song—a fragment of a voice torn from its source. Martin touched one, and a pang of grief ran through him.

"We can't leave them here," he said. "We need to break these cages."

But breaking a container would destroy the voice. We needed a key—the resonance that could unlock them all. I opened myself to the song, letting the grief of a thousand worlds pour through me, and I sang.

It was not a melody I knew. It was a harmony of pure compassion. The crystals began to glow in response, pulsing in time with my voice. Then, one by one, they began to crack—not breaking, but opening like flowers.
