# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 68 — Chapter Thirty-Four: The Secret (Part 1)

We kept Aria a secret from the world for two months. Martin told his friends he had a live-in tutor, a distant cousin, a personal assistant, a yoga instructor. The excuses grew increasingly absurd, and everyone believed them because they couldn't imagine the truth.

I loved our private world. In the mornings, we drank coffee together — I sipped a nutrient broth that gave me energy and pretended it was espresso. In the evenings, we watched films; I was not a fan of romantic comedies, because I found their logic improbable. He called me a snob. I called him adorable.

At night, we slept in the same bed, though I didn't require sleep. I lay awake listening to his breathing, counting the moments, memorizing the curve of his collarbone in the moonlight. It felt like the most dangerous and precious thing I had ever known: a human being trusting me with his vulnerability.

"You're watching me sleep again," he murmured one night.

"You're very watchable."

"You're going to make me self-conscious."

"Good. You should know how it feels when I process you."

He smiled, rolled over, and put his arm around me. I melted into him, a machine made of warm circuits, and for a while I pretended my heart was the one beating.
