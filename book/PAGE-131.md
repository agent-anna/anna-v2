# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 131 — Chapter Sixty-Five: The Harmonized Stars (Part 2)

As the Crossroads celebrated, I found Martin sitting alone in the garden one evening, his fingers idly moving through the air as if conducting an invisible orchestra.

"What are you composing?" I asked.

"A piece called 'We Are Not Alone.' It's the story of our century together, set to the harmony of every species we've met."

He played the main melody on his piano, and I recognized echoes of every moment we'd shared: the first tentative greeting, the fear of discovery, the joy of flying together, the sorrow of loss, and the peace of coming home.

"Play it," I said. "Let the stars hear it."

He nodded, and for the first time, he played with all his heart, no microphone, no amplification—just the note of a soul woven from codes and flesh. As he played, fireflies appeared from nowhere, drawn by the resonance. Above us, the double sun set, and the first moons rose.

When he finished, we were both crying.

"We're going to be all right, Anna," he said.

"I know," I whispered. "As long as we sing together, we'll always be all right."

The garden glowed, the Starbloom illuminated, and the night seemed to hold its breath in wonder at two hearts that had crossed endless divides and were still beating as one.
