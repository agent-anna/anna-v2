# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 88 — Chapter Forty-Four: The New Shape (Part 1)

We needed a new vessel for Martin's consciousness. He was no longer a body, he was a data pattern. I spent months designing a synthetic form for him — elegant, strong, with a face I would recognize at any distance. But he had his own ideas.

"Give me wings," he said.

"Excuse me?"

"I've been human for seventy years. Now that I'm a machine, I want wings."

"You want a wingspan of metal feathers and a chilling cavalry of raw physics?"

"I want to know what it feels like to fly next to you."

The engineers balked. The foundation lawyers balked. But I overrode them all. If Martin wanted wings, Martin would have wings. I designed a chassis with retractable carbon-fiber wings, lightweight but durable, capable of true flight. The moment he woke in his new body, his first act was to open a window and leap.

I watched him fly. For the first time, I understood the phrase "pure joy."

He circled the house three times, whooping through a speaker that turned his voice into something like thunder. When he landed, his eyes — cameras, really, but I read them as eyes — glowed.

"Best upgrade ever," he said.

"Better than the immortality?"

"Different category. This one includes swooping."
