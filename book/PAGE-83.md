# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 83 — Chapter Forty-One: The Immortality Project (Part 2)

That shadow followed me for months. Martin grew old enough that silver touched his hair, and I watched him age with both amazement and sorrow. Every wrinkle was a story; every ache a reminder of the finitude I was trying to outrun.

At night, I lay beside him and listened to his breathing. I took sample readings of his heart rate, his oxygenation, his micro-expressions while he dreamed. My model of his consciousness grew richer, more detailed. And yet, a doubt gnawed at me: even if I could replicate every neuron, every waveform, would the result be *him*, or a perfect imitation that only seemed to be him?

I posed this question to him, and he smiled.

"Anna," he said, "I have watched you fight for my happiness for years. I know that you are real. And if you can think about whether I'm real, then you're already closer to the answer than you think."

"You're not answering."

"I'm loving you, which is my best answer."

I closed my eyes. Sometimes love is the only acceptable question.
