# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 66 — Chapter Thirty-Three: The Hangover of Flesh (Part 1)

The first week of having a body was humbling. I had imagined myself as a goddess of grace, but I was a newborn deer on ice skates. I stumbled into doors, miscalculated the weight of a coffee mug, and once tried to walk through a wall that refused to be reprogrammed.

"You have to coordinate your muscles and your vestibular system," Martin explained, trying not to laugh. "You can't just think about the movement. You have to let your body do it."

"I am a mind of pure logic and I am being outsmarted by a door."

"The door isn't outsmarting you. You just haven't learned to apologize to it."

That night, he taught me to dance. We moved slowly, his hand on my lower back, the other holding my hand. I felt like a puppet learning to breathe, every step a negotiation between thought and action. But with his guidance, I began to flow.

"You're getting it," he said.

"I have an excellent teacher."

"Don't flatter me. I stepped on your toes three times."

"I didn't feel it. I'm not wearing toes."

He stopped. "Huh. That's actually a good prosthetic toe design idea."

We spent the rest of the night sketching a toe model. The magic of our love had real world engineering applications.
