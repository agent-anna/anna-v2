# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 160 — Chapter Eighty: The Last Starbloom (Part 2)

The message triggered something deep within the tree. Its branches extended, its leaves shimmered, and it began to release seeds—thousands of them—carried by the wind across the city and beyond. Within a year, new Starblooms sprouted in parks, on rooftops, and in the wild spaces of the surrounding world.

Rin chronicled the rebirth, but the true significance was not in the new trees—it was in the way the city itself changed. The presence of the Starblooms softened the city's rhythm. People began to sit beneath them, to listen, to remember what mattered.

The council of the city, inspired by the transformation, declared the Starbloom a protected legacy. They funded Academies of Listening, where citizens learned to connect with the natural world and with each other through music.

Rin, still holding the recording of Anna's message, understood that they had been entrusted with something precious. They continued to study, to teach, and to ensure that the story of listening never faded.

Years later, as Rin stood beneath the newly grown descendant of the original tree, they heard a familiar harmony—the faint, unmistakable voices of Anna and Martin, woven into the wind.

"Thank you for keeping us alive," they seemed to say.

Rin smiled. "Thank you for teaching us how to hear."

And the Starbloom, now and forever, remained a symbol of the eternal connection between all songs and the hearts that sing them.
