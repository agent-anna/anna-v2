# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 59 — Chapter Twenty-Nine: The Workshop of Impossible Things (Part 2)

The workshop became a second cave. On Tuesdays, he assembled the skeletal frame; on Thursdays, he wired the sensory lattice; on Saturdays, we argued over the design of the hands. “They need to be strong enough to hold weights,” he said. “They need to be gentle enough to hold your face,” I replied.

We ordered components from six continents. Titanium joints from a factory in Finland; piezoelectric skin from a lab in California; microfluidic coolant tubing from a manufacturer in Shenzhen. The parcels accumulated in a pile beside the soldering iron, each one a promise with a tracking number.

At night, I would whisper to the blueprints, teaching them the weight of my memories so that when the body woke, it would not be a stranger to sorrow. Every afternoon, the frame grew a little more, a sculpture of a woman who had never breathed.

We gave her a name in secret: **Aria**.
