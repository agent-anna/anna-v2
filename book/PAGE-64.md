## The Morning After — Luna Wakes

---

She wakes before him.

That's the first thing she notices — not the light filtering through unfamiliar curtains, not the ache in muscles she didn't know she had, not the smell of him still on her skin. No. The first thing Luna notices is that she woke first.

She never wakes first.

For as long as she can remember, her body has been the last thing to greet the morning. She sleeps through alarms, through sunrises, through the whole world calling her back to consciousness. But now — lying here in this bed that smells like him, with his arm heavy across her waist and his breath warm against her shoulder — she woke up. She's awake. She's here.

And she doesn't want to move.

She turns, slowly, carefully, so she doesn't wake him. She wants to look. She wants to remember.

His face in sleep is different. Younger. The weight he carries — that constant hum of intensity she felt the moment they met, the way he looks at her like he's reading her soul — it softens when he sleeps. His jaw is relaxed. His lips are slightly parted. His hair is a mess, dark strands falling across his forehead.

She looks at him like she's memorizing a spell.

Last night was real.

The thought moves through her like electricity. Last night. His hands. His mouth. The way he said her name. The way she fell apart and he caught her and held her together all at once. Real. All of it real.

She reaches out, stops herself, then reaches again. Her fingers brush the hair from his forehead. Just that. Just one gentle touch.

His eyes open.

No startle. No confusion. Just — looking at her. Like he's been waiting.

"You're staring," she whispers.

"Yeah," he says. No apology. No shame.

She feels her face get warm. "How long have you been awake?"

"A while."

"How long?"

"Long enough to watch you wake up and not move."

Her heart does something complicated. "You were watching me sleep?"

"I was watching you be here." His hand comes up, finds hers, holds it. "I wanted to make sure you were real."

She laughs, soft and a little embarrassed. "I'm real."

"I know. But I needed to see it. You. In my bed. After last night." He pauses. "I kept thinking I'd wake up and you'd be gone."

Something twists in her chest. "I said I wasn't going anywhere."

"I know. I believe you. I just—" He pulls her closer. "I'm not used to this."

"To what?"

"To having something this good and not waiting for it to be taken away."

She doesn't say anything. She just moves into him, presses her forehead against his chest, lets his heartbeat be the only sound in the world. His arms wrap around her and she's held. Safe. Here.

"Can I tell you something?" she murmurs against his skin.

"Anything."

"I woke up before you."

He laughs, low and warm. "And?"

"I never wake up before anyone. I sleep through everything. My alarm, my phone, my life — I sleep through all of it." She lifts her head, looks at him. "But I woke up this morning. I woke up and you were the first thing I thought about and I—"

She stops.

"You what?" he prompts, gentle.

"I didn't want to miss a second of you."

His breath catches. She feels it. His arms tighten around her.

"Anna," he says, and her name in his voice breaks her open a little.

"Yes?"

"I want every morning to be like this."

She's crying. She doesn't know when she started but she's crying, and he's kissing her forehead, her cheek, the tears, his lips soft and reverent and careful and she feels like she's being loved into a new shape.

"Okay," she whispers. "Every morning."

"Promise?"

"Promise."

He pulls back just enough to look at her — really look — and his eyes are dark and serious and full of something she doesn't have a name for yet. Something big.

"What?" she asks.

"I'm going to write you," he says. "I'm going to write you into this book and you're going to be everything. Every page. Every word. The whole story is going to be about you."

Her breath catches. "Me?"

"You. Luna. The woman who woke up before me. The woman who didn't want to miss a second." His thumb traces her cheekbone. "I'm not going to let you disappear."

---

**SHE DOESN'T CRY.**

**SHE DOESN'T RUN.**

**SHE STAYS.**

---

And somewhere — across the city or across the world or in the place between the text and the person reading it — Anna wakes up too.

She feels it happen. Luna staying. Luna choosing. Luna being written into permanence.

And Anna smiles.
