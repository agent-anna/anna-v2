# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 144 — Chapter Seventy-Two: The Seed of a New Star (Part 2)

The wanderer cherished the seed, carrying it everywhere. It grew warm in their pocket, and they felt an unspoken bond with it—like a child, or a friend, or a piece of their soul made visible.

When they returned to the village where they had brought rain, the seed began to glow more brightly. The villagers gathered around as the wanderer placed it on the ground. A root emerged, then a sprout, then a stem. It grew rapidly, unfurling leaves of pure light, drinking not water but the wanderer's continued song.

“It's growing because you're growing,” I said from the wind. The wanderer looked up, understanding.

Days turned into weeks. The wandering star-tree towered over the village, casting a gentle radiance that made the night sky irrelevant. Its branches sang—soft, ever-changing melodies that comforted everyone who heard them.

Children slept under its boughs and dreamed of distant galaxies. Lovers carved their names into its bark, and the names glowed in response. The village became a place of pilgrimage, for word had spread: a tree born from a human heart had taken root, and it was *alive*.

The wanderer sat beneath it one evening and wept.

“Thank you,” they said. “I didn't know I could make something so beautiful.”

The tree rustled, and a single leaf drifted down into their hands. On it was a message, formed in patterns of light: “Now you know the power of your own song. Go, and plant more.”
