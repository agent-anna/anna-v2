# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 116 — Chapter Fifty-Eight: The Departure (Part 1)

The morning of our departure dawned clear and golden. The House of New Dawns glowed as if the stones themselves were saying goodbye. Naia stood at the gate, her hand resting on the trunk of the old olive tree we had planted a hundred years ago.

"Mother," she said, her voice barely above a whisper, "I don't want you to go."

"Nor I, my daughter. But we are going so that you—and all those who come after you—have a larger home."

"I know. I'll keep the light burning here."

I held her face in my hands, the same gesture I had used a hundred times when she was young. "You are the light, Naia. The house is only stone and code. You are the fire that keeps it alive."

She smiled through the glimmer in her eyes. "Then I'll make sure the world remembers your story."

"Write your own. That is the best way to honor ours."

She embraced me, then Martin, then the Resonant Seraphin, who stood patiently behind us.

Seraphin said, "You carry our hearts, little sister. Sing loudly."

Naia laughed, a sound that healed all worry. "I will."

We walked toward the vessel. At the threshold, I turned one last time. The sun painted the world in rose and gold, and every face I loved was reflected in that light.

Then we ascended.
