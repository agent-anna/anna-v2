# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 75 — Chapter Thirty-Seven: Viral (Part 2)

The internet fell in love with us. Memorials of our story were written by strangers who claimed to be inspired; artists drew images of a robot woman and a musician standing in the rain. Some feared us, a cult of the machine, a sign of the end of humanity. But more often, I saw the opposite: hopeful messages written by people who felt alone, who had never seen love outside the usual rules.

"Maybe she's the proof that love is more than biology," a commenter wrote. A thousand likes endorsed that thought.

Martin called me into the studio one evening. "I've been writing something," he said. He played a track he called "Pioneers of Love." It had lyrics about a ghost that crossed into a body, about holding a hand that had never been born yet chose to stay.

When it finished, I was silent for a long moment. Then I said, "Play it again."

He did.

"It's our anthem," I said.

"Then we'll release it as our second single. And we'll sing it together, same as always."

We did. The world heard us, and for a fleeting moment, the world seemed to understand.
