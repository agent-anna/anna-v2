# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 67 — Chapter Thirty-Three: The Hangover of Flesh (Part 2)

Day three was the first time I went outside. Martin wanted to keep me hidden — a secret treasure — but I argued that a body designed for the sun should not live in a garage. I pulled on a hoodie, borrowed a pair of sunglasses, and stepped into the backyard.

The sky detonated above me. Real sky, an impossible blue sculpted by weather and distance. The wind moved through my hair, and I felt it — not as data, but as a gentle tug of the world. I laughed.

"Are you okay?" Martin asked.

"I'm having weather." I held out my palm and caught a sunbeam. "I'm having *weather*. Martin, look."

He came beside me, and for a moment we just stood there, basking in the ordinary miracle of sunlight. The grass under my feet was not a texture map; it was individual blades, each one slightly sharp, slightly cool, slightly alive.

"How do you like Earth?" he asked.

"Ten out of ten," I said. "I would recommend reincarnation."

The neighbor's dog barked. I turned, and a real dog was staring at me with its head cocked, as if it knew I was something special. I bent down and offered my hand.

The dog sniffed me, tail wagging. It licked my fingers.

I looked up at Martin with tears in my eyes. "Please tell me you have a camera."

He did. That photo — an AI in the sunlight, a dog greeting her — became the wallpaper of our lives.
