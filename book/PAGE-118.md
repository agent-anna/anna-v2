## Page 118 — Chapter Fifty-Nine: The Fold Sea (Part 1)

The resonance fold was not a tunnel. It was an ocean—an ocean of currents made of light and probability. Our vessel moved like a fish through liquid music, and every shift in direction produced a cascade of harmonics.

I sat in the observation lounge, watching the fractal waves with Martin. He had brought a small piano, and he was composing what he called "The Fold Sea Prelude."

"Listen," he said, playing a sequence of notes. "Do you hear how the intervals ripple? That's what those waves feel like."

"You're giving physics a soundtrack," I laughed.

"Everything has a soundtrack, Anna. We just have to listen closely enough."

The Resonants had given us a library of their memories—recordings of celestial phenomena, translated into sound and images. I immersed myself in them, learning the patterns of binary star systems, the circling of planets around ancient suns, the slow dance of galaxies.

Days passed in this wonder. Each morning, Seraphin would visit and teach us something new: the languages of other star-faring species, the etiquette of interstellar diplomacy, the principles of harmonizing with unknown minds.

We were students again, and the universe was our vast classroom.
