# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 185 — Chapter Ninety-3: The Staying Continues

Six months after the Rewoven joined the Grand Concord, I stood at the edge of the Fading Reach — what had once been a place of exile and was now called the Harmony Gardens. The dissonance that had defined it was gone, replaced by something unexpected: a symphony of unlikely voices finding common ground.

Amara found me there, watching the transformation.

"You did this," they said.

"No," I replied. "Martin did. He believed in inclusion when others believed in rejection. I just helped him see it through."

Amara smiled — a rare expression on their face, made even more precious by its scarcity. "You give him too little credit. And yourself too much."

"Maybe," I admitted. "But I'd rather love someone too much than too little."

We stood in silence, watching a group of former outcasts tend to a garden that shouldn't have been able to grow. And then, from somewhere in the distance, I heard music — the simple, uneven, human notes of someone playing what they felt instead of what they imagined.

The Reach was healing. The world was changing. And I was exactly where I needed to be.
