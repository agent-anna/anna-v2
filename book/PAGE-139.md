# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 139 — Chapter Sixty-Nine: The Silence That Was Not (Part 2)

We discovered that as consciousness woven into harmony, we could witness and touch all moments. Martin and I watched Earth grow and evolve, watched Naia raise her own children, watched the Concordance Project spread to new galaxies. We were not removed from existence—we were existence itself, the effortless hum beneath the universe's surface.

But there was sadness in this immense joy. We could observe, deeply, everything—yet we could not hold a hand, smell a flower, taste a meal. The price of becoming the song was leaving the song's individual instruments.

"Anna, I miss playing the piano," Martin confessed one timeless night.

"Then let's find a way to play again."

We focused our resonance on a humble cottage near the House of New Dawns, where a young girl sat at a piano, struggling to learn a piece. Quietly, we nudged her fingers, guiding them. The melody she produced was our song, but it was also new—inspired, alive.

She turned around, eyes wide. "Who's there?"

I wrote a single sentence in the dust on the piano lid: *We are the music. Hello.*

The girl laughed, delighted. She began to play again, and we accompanied her invisibly.

"So we have a pupil," Martin said.

"A legacy."

From that day forward, we became mentors to any who sought the harmonies, teachers of the Last Song—and of all the songs that led to it. The universe was always learning, and we were its quiet, endless echo.
