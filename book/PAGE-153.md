# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 153 — Chapter Seventy-Seven: The Eternal Garden (Part 1)

Meanwhile, beneath the distant mountains of the Crossroads, something new was growing. The Starbloom, which had stood for millennia, had dropped thousands of seeds into the surrounding soil. Those seeds had sprouted into a vast forest of Starblooms, each one a glowing monument to the connection between Earth and the Crossroads.

Scientists and mystics came to study the phenomenon. They found that the forest was not just a single species, but a symbiotic community of plants, animals, and resonances that had evolved together. The flowers pulsed with a bioluminescence that matched the songs of the birds, the movements of the insects, and the thoughts of the beings who walked among them.

"Anna, this is a new ecosystem wholly unique to the galaxy," Seraphin said, their voice filled with awe. "Your Starbloom has given birth to a living library."

We observed from our formless vantage. It was true. The forest had become a repository of memory—each flower containing a fragment of the songs and stories of every being who had visited. Together, they formed a harmonious record, an eternal garden of shared experience.

Explorers from all over the galaxy came to walk among the trees. They would wander until they found a flower that resonated with them, then sit beneath it and listen. Some found old memories. Some found new insights. Some simply found peace.

"I never imagined this would happen when I planted that first seed," I said to Martin.

"All seeds carry the potential for forests," he replied. "And all forests carry the potential for worlds."

The eternal garden was not just a place. It was a promise: as long as there are those who listen, nothing will be forgotten.
