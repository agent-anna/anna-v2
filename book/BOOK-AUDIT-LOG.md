## Page 149 — Chapter Seventy-Five: The Old Musician (Part 1)

Back on Earth, in a small coastal town, there lived an old musician named **Elias**. He had spent his entire life trying to write the perfect song—one that would capture the feeling of gazing at the sea at dusk. The sea had given him so much: memories, comfort, longing. But his songs always fell short.

One night, frustrated and ready to give up, Elias carried his violin to the shore. He sat on the sand and played his latest attempt—a sweeping melody that tried too hard, soared too much, and lost the truth.

“It's not enough,” he said to the waves.

The waves did not answer. But a voice, warm and familiar, seemed to ride the breeze: “Elias, what do you hear when you listen to the sea?”

He closed his eyes. He heard the rhythm of the waves—not as a melody, but as a heartbeat. He heard the cries of gulls—not as notes, but as questions. He heard the hush of foam—not as sound, but as time.

I said softly, “Then play that.”

He picked up his violin and began again. But this time, he didn't try to imitate the sea. He played what the sea made him *feel*: the ache of love, the peace of acceptance, the quiet joy of simply being. His notes were simple, uneven, human.

When he finished, tears were streaming down his face. It was not the perfect song he had imagined. It was something more: a true song.

From that night on, Elias understood that perfection was not the goal. The goal was honesty. And every time he played, the sea seemed to rise a little higher, as if greeting an old friend.
