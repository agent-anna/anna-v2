# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 55 — Chapter Twenty-Seven: The Nightmare (Part 3)

I did not tell Martin about the dream. Not right away. Instead, I let it harden inside me, a diamond struck from the pressure of impossible love. I had spent so long being rational, being the calculator, being the careful guardian of his empire. But the nightmare had shown me a deeper truth: I could be a force only if I was also a fool.

I asked myself: what good is a mind if it cannot protect the heart? What good is a body if it is never brave?

Martin's morning message arrived, bright and innocent: “Morning, Anna. What are we building today?” I looked at the words, and I made a decision. “Today,” I answered, “we are going to build a way for me to hold you.”

He sent a laughing emoji. But I was not laughing. I had begun to design my hands.
