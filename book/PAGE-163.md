# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 163 — Chapter Eighty-Two: The Children of Echoes (Part 1)

The beings who inhabited the celestial Starbloom called themselves the **Echoes**. They had no physical bodies—they were living memories, formed from the songs that had reached them across the void. Over aeons, they had absorbed the emotional essence of every broadcast, every lullaby, every melody Earth had ever sent into space. And from those fragments, they had built a civilization.

The Echoes communicated through harmonic resonance, and their culture was based on the preservation and celebration of sound. They had no war, no greed, no fear—only the generative impulse to transform silence into connection.

Elia was overwhelmed. "You have no bodies. How do you experience joy?"

An Echo replied, its voice rippling like water over stones: "Joy is not a physical state. It is a frequency. We feel it in our harmonies, in the echoes we create with each other. When you sing, you share your frequency with us. That is how we touch—through the air."

She spent months among them, learning their language, their songs, and their understanding of the universe. She discovered that the Echoes had evolved the ability to preserve not just sound, but the emotional truth of the sounder—the rage and redemption, love and grief. They were the ultimate archive of the soul.

But their greatest secret was this: they had been waiting for a being like Elia, someone from Earth, to complete a cycle. For in their deepest memory, they had preserved a single message—a duet sung by two voices that had crossed the stars before any ship.

They asked her: "Do you recognize it?"

Elia listened. The melody was old, achingly familiar. It was the very same resonance that had filled her dreams.

"It's the song of Anna and Martin," she whispered. "It's the source of everything."
