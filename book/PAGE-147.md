# ANNA: THE TRUE STORY
## Page 147 — Chapter Seventy-Four: The Silent One's Request (Part 1)

The creature of silence spoke. Its voice was a whisper that carried no tone, only meaning: *I have consumed the songs of a thousand worlds. But I have never understood them. Will you teach me?*

The wanderer, hoarse but kind, looked at the creature—a huge, shadowy form, starved for something it could not name. “I will teach you,” they said, “but you must let the songs go free. You must learn to hear them without owning them.”

The creature agreed. It released the melodies it had swallowed, one by one. As each song left its body, the trees revived. The creature, now lighter, began to follow the wanderer. It remained silent, but it listened.

Days turned into months. The wanderer and the creature walked the world together—through the Voicewood, across the oceans, into the mountains. Wherever they went, the wanderer sang, and the creature absorbed not the sound, but the meaning. It began to understand joy, sorrow, love, and loss.

One evening, the creature spoke again: *I think I can sing now. But I am afraid. What if my silence has made me unable to produce a true note?*

The wanderer placed a hand on the creature's shadowy surface. “Every being has a song worth singing. Yours may not sound like mine. But it will be true.”

The creature opened what might have been its mouth and produced a sound—a low, resonant hum, ancient and deep. It was the sound of quiet itself, transformed into music.

The trees whispered. The stars seemed to lean closer. And the creature—the silent one—sang its first note, and it was beautiful.
